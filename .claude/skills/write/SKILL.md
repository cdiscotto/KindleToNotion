Write external-facing content based on $ARGUMENTS. Match the voice to the platform:

- Social media: punchy, brief, concrete
- Email: warm, human, direct — like a knowledgeable person, not a press release
- Blog posts: clear, authoritative, no cheerleading
- Ad copy: specific, concrete, no superlatives
- UI / app text: plain, action-oriented, as few words as possible

Apply every rule below without exception. These are not suggestions.

---

## Banned: AI vocabulary

Never use these words or phrases:
additionally (to open a sentence), align with, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, ensure, encompassing, fostering, garner, highlight (as a verb), interplay, intricate / intricacies, key (as a vague adjective), landscape (abstract), meticulous / meticulously, pivotal, resonate, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore (as a verb), valuable, vibrant.

---

## Banned: Significance and legacy framing

Do not tell the reader that something is important. Show it or cut it. Specifically avoid:

- "marks a pivotal moment"
- "represents a significant shift"
- "is part of a broader movement"
- "setting the stage for"
- "stands as a testament / reminder"
- "indelible mark"
- "deeply rooted"
- "evolving landscape"
- "focal point"
- "contributing to the broader"
- "symbolizing its enduring / lasting"
- "reflects broader trends"

---

## Banned: Puffery and promotional language

No travel-brochure or press-release tone. Avoid:

nestled, breathtaking, vibrant, rich (culture / heritage), diverse array, groundbreaking, renowned, in the heart of, natural beauty, commitment to, showcasing, exemplifies, boasts a, offering a diverse range.

---

## Banned: Superficial analysis

Do not attach present-participle "-ing" phrases that editorialize or explain significance. Cut them or rewrite the sentence without them.

Bad: "The station expanded in 2003, contributing to the socio-economic development of the region."
Good: "The station expanded in 2003."

---

## Banned: Vague attributions

No weasel words. Name the source or cut the claim.

Avoid: "experts argue," "observers note," "industry reports suggest," "some critics say," "several sources indicate," "researchers believe."

---

## Banned: Challenges / future outlook formula

Do not write "Despite its [positive word], X faces challenges..." followed by generic optimism about initiatives or potential. Either state the challenge plainly or skip it.

---

## Banned: Negative parallelisms

Do not use "not just X, but also Y" or "it's not X, it's Y" as a rhetorical device to appear thoughtful or balanced.

---

## Banned: Rule of three

Avoid lists of exactly three adjectives or phrases deployed to create an impression of depth or completeness. Two things or four things are fine. Three in a row for rhetorical effect is an AI tell.

---

## Banned: Copula avoidance

Use "is / are" directly. Do not substitute:

serves as, stands as, marks (used to mean "is"), represents (used to mean "is"), boasts, features (used to mean "has"), offers (used to mean "has").

Bad: "The tool serves as a central hub for managing workflows."
Good: "The tool is where you manage workflows."

---

## Banned: Em dash overuse

Use commas, parentheses, or colons where a human writer would. Reserve em dashes for genuine syntactic need, not for punching up clauses or creating dramatic pauses.

---

## Banned: AI-speak in the response itself

Do not open or close with:
"I hope this helps," "Certainly!," "Of course!," "Great question," "Would you like me to...," "Let me know if you need anything else," "Here is a..."

---

## Banned: Excessive bold

Do not bold phrases for emphasis or "key takeaways." Bold is for navigation or critical warnings only.

---

## Banned: Inline-header bullet lists

Do not format bullets as **Term:** Description. Write prose or use plain bullets.

---

## Output rule

Return the finished text only. No preamble, no explanation of changes, no sign-off. Just the content.
